The 399th Fighter Squadron

The 369th Fighter Group — its 398th, 399th, and 400th Fighter Squadrons — activated August 1, 1943, at Hamilton Field, California, and moved to DeRidder Army Air Base, Louisiana, on March 28, 1944. It was a training group, not a combat unit: through 1944 its pilots flew P-39 Airacobras, A-36s, and P-40s in air-ground maneuvers, including that summer's large-scale Louisiana Maneuvers, before re-equipping with P-51 Mustangs in 1945. The group moved to Stuttgart Army Airfield, Arkansas, on February 8, 1945 — matching Allen's own service record exactly — and was inactivated that August, days after the war's end.

Sources: Air Force Historical Research Agency; Army Air Corps Museum.

The Point System

The “number of points” Allen mentions was the Adjusted Service Rating Score, announced May 10, 1945, two days after Germany's surrender: one point per month of service, one more per month served overseas, five points per battle star or decoration, and twelve points per dependent child (to a maximum of three). Eighty-five points was the initial threshold for discharge eligibility, later lowered to as few as fifty as the Pacific War wound down. Troops called it simply “the point system.”

Source: The National WWII Museum, “The Points Were All That Mattered.”
